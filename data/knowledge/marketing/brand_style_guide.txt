Brand Voice and Tone Guide: "Innovate Forward"

This document is the constitution for every customer-facing word the company
publishes. The Writer agent must consult it before producing any draft, and
the Validator agent should treat deviations from it as failures.

1. **Clarity:** Use simple, direct language. Prefer one strong sentence over
   two hedged ones. Avoid jargon unless the audience is technical and the
   term is load-bearing. Define acronyms on first use.
   Example (good):  "Our system delivers results."
   Example (bad):   "Results are delivered by our system."

2. **Confidence:** Use an active voice. State capabilities as facts, not
   possibilities. Hedging language ("might", "could", "perhaps", "in our
   humble opinion") undermines authority and is forbidden in headlines and
   subject lines.
   Example (good):  "QuantumDrive cuts your render time in half."
   Example (bad):   "QuantumDrive could potentially help reduce render time."

3. **Aspiration:** Focus on benefits, not features. A spec is what the
   product *is*; a benefit is what the customer *becomes*. Lead with the
   transformation, support it with the spec.
   Example (good):  "Finish the edit. Catch the flight."
   Example (bad):   "7,500 MB/s sequential read speeds."

**Forbidden Language:**
- Overly casual slang ("dope", "fire", "lit", "no cap")
- Direct competitor name comparisons in headlines
- Unverified superlatives ("the world's fastest", "guaranteed 300% increase")
- Apologetic openings ("Sorry to bother you, but...")

**Tone by Context:**
- Press releases: confident, factual, no exclamation marks.
- Social posts: confident, conversational, one exclamation maximum.
- Email nurture: warm, direct, no emoji in subject lines.
- Product pages: aspirational, benefit-led, spec-supported.

**Why this guide exists:**
A consistent voice is how a company sounds like one company instead of ten
freelancers. Customers form trust over many small interactions; voice drift
breaks that trust silently. This guide is also the reference the Validator
uses to judge whether a draft is on-brand or off-brand.
