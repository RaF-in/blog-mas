Customer Interview Notes — Maria R.
Date: 2026-02-12
Interviewer: Priya S. (UX research)
Format: 45-minute remote, recorded with consent
Participant: Maria R., freelance colorist, Lisbon, 9 years experience

PARTICIPANT CONTEXT
Maria works on commercial spots and short documentaries. Typical project
involves 8K BRAW footage and DaVinci Resolve color grading. Solo operator.
Currently uses two Samsung 980 Pro 2TB drives in a Thunderbolt enclosure.

VERBATIM QUOTES (selected)

On her current pain points:
"The wait is what kills me. I scrub the timeline and it stutters. Every
stutter pulls me out of the work. I lose the eye for what I was doing."

"I bill by the project, not the hour. So 'faster drive' isn't a luxury for
me, it's directly more money. Or directly more sleep. Either is fine."

On what she would actually pay for:
"Honestly the spec sheet doesn't sell me. I want to see another colorist
say 'I edited this Apple commercial on it and the timeline never dropped a
frame.' That's the thing."

"Endurance scares me more than speed now. My last drive died at 18 months
and I lost two days of work. I want a number I can trust for five years."

On marketing language she ignores:
"Anything with 'unleash' or 'revolutionary'. I scroll past. I want
specifics. Numbers. A real workflow."

"Brands that compare themselves to other brands look insecure. Just tell
me what *you* do."

On price sensitivity:
"I'd pay $200 more for a drive I don't have to think about. The cheap
drive isn't cheap if it costs me a Saturday rebuilding a project."

INTERVIEWER NOTES
- Maria is the prototype "studio-of-one" persona. Strong signal that
  endurance and reliability beat raw speed in purchase intent.
- The "show me a working pro using it" desire is a campaign hint:
  testimonials > benchmarks for this segment.
- She rejected hyperbolic copy unprompted. Good independent confirmation
  of the brand style guide's "no overly casual slang / no unverified
  superlatives" rules.
- Her question "what's the TBW for the 4TB" went unanswered on a
  competitor's product page. Make sure ours surfaces it.
